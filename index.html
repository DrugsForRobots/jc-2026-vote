The 2026 Judicial Committee Vote

Grand Rapids, May 24–25, 2026: one round, two seats, five appointed

Delegates elected two of seven Judicial Committee members on a single ballot. The chair ruled that a second round needed a two-thirds vote to suspend the rules. Over two days, every attempt at one failed, and the two winners appointed the other five. No vote ever asked the delegates whether the rules already required a second round, so that question is still contested.

How The Rules Connect

Where The Rules Run Out

The Judicial Committee has no voting procedure of its own. It borrows the at-large one, and that one stops after a single ballot.

Bylaws Art. 8.1

Seven Members, Elected Every Four Years

The Judicial Committee shall be composed of seven Party members elected at each regular non-presidential convention.

Convention Rule 9

Borrow The At-Large Method

Elections run in the same manner as specified for at-large members of the National Committee.

Convention Rule 8.2

One Approval Ballot, A Majority Of Ballots To Win

Each delegate votes for any number of candidates. The top five candidates receiving a majority vote of the ballots cast shall be elected.

The Gap

Rule 8.2 Says Nothing About A Second Ballot

Officers get runoffs written into Rule 8.1c, and presidential nominees into Rule 7.2. At-large and Judicial Committee seats get no such sentence.

Reading 1 · The Chair’s

Rule 8.2 Is Complete. Another Ballot Needs 2/3 To Suspend The Rules.

Ruled the second-round point of order NOT WELL TAKEN on the basis that no such requirement exists in the bylaws. The 2020 Bylaws Committee read it the same way, describing a lack of a procedure to automatically move to additional rounds of voting without a need to suspend the rules.

2026 minutes p.32 · 2020 minutes p.174
Reading 2 · Robert’s Fills The Gap

Art. 16 Applies Robert’s, & Robert’s Repeats The Ballot.

Where LP rules are silent, Robert’s governs, and if any office remains unfilled after the first ballot, the balloting is repeated for that office as many times as necessary to obtain a majority vote for a single candidate. An election should be completed at the session at which it is taken up, unless it is impossible or impractical to do so.

Art. 16 · RONR 46:32, 46:44

Precedent: in 2014, the first election under this rule, four LNC at-large candidates won majorities and two tied for the fifth seat at 45.48%. The delegates filled it themselves: Mr. Redpath moved to suspend the requirements of Convention Rule 8.2 and to instead fill the fifth at-large position by coin toss between Doug Craig and Guy McLendon, though they did not receive majority votes; the motion passed on a voice vote. 2014 minutes p.43

On The Floor

How The Question Was Closed

In the order the minutes record it, from the round-one results on Sunday through the last attempt on Monday. Where a detail comes only from Ken Moellman’s own account, the step says so; he is one of the two members who made the appointments.

Sunday, May 24

Two Declared Elected

ELECTED

Having received a majority of the 610 ballots cast, Ken Moellman and Avens O'Brien were ELECTED to the Judicial Committee.

Point Of Order: Go To A Second Round

NOT WELL TAKEN

Paul Bracco’s point was ruled against on the basis that no such requirement exists in the bylaws.

Motion To Suspend The Rules For A Second Round

FAILED

Aaron Starr’s motion needed two-thirds. It failed by a show of hands, with no count recorded.

Point Of Order: The Rules Don’t Conflict With Robert’s

NOT WELL TAKEN

Nick Ciesielski’s point was ruled against on the basis that a motion to suspend the rules to move to a second round of voting had already failed, which doesn’t address whether Robert’s already required one. By Moellman’s account, he appealed the ruling twice; on each occasion the chair answered that this was "not a conversation," and neither appeal was put. Moellman’s own view: My personal opinion is that the initial challenge to the chair's ruling made by Mr. Ciesielski should have been heard by the body.

Moellman pp.3–4

Appeal From The Chair’s Ruling

RULED DILATORY

Paul Bracco (VA) APPEALED from the ruling of the Chair. Chair Nekhaila ruled the motion dilatory and OUT OF ORDER. The delegates never voted on the appeal. Robert’s gives any two members the right to appeal.

2026 minutes pp.32–33 · RONR 24:1

Monday, May 25

Resolution: Delegates Elect The Next Five

OUT OF ORDER

Paul Bracco moved to suspend the rules for a resolution electing the five candidates ranked third through seventh. The chair first ruled it in order, then, after an appeal and a talk with the parliamentarian, reversed himself: Chair Nekhaila ruled the resolution OUT OF ORDER because voting was ongoing.

2026 minutes pp.46–47

Motion To Suspend The Rules For A Second Round

FAILED

Alicia Percell’s motion. The chair ruled it in order, as the motion was a valid motion to suspend the rules and reconsider something previously adopted. It failed on a show of hands, then: Division was called for and the motion FAILED by a standing vote. No count is recorded.

2026 minutes pp.47–48

Appeal: Seat The Next Five

CHAIR SUSTAINED

Andrew Chadderdon moved to seat the top five from the first round. The chair ruled it out of order as dilatory (Moellman says, as too similar to Sunday’s motion), and on appeal, The ruling of the Chair was SUSTAINED by a counted vote of 285-192. Moellman adds that this vote does not establish the view of those delegates on the merits of seating additional members.

Draft minutes p.49 · Moellman pp.5–6

The Two Winners Fill Five Seats

CONTESTED

Under Art. 8.1, The remaining members of the Judicial Committee shall appoint new members if vacancies occur. The full committee later upheld New Hampshire’s revocation by a vote of 7 to 0.

JC opinion, LPNH, p.1

Earlier Conventions

Twenty Years Of Seating Both Races

Rule 9 runs the Judicial Committee election in the same manner as specified for at-large members of the National Committee, so the two races belong side by side. Both elect every seat at once: all five at-large seats at each regular convention, every two years (Art. 7.2–7.3, Art. 10.1), and all seven Judicial Committee seats at each non-presidential one (Art. 8.1). Empty at-large seats go to the LNC: The National Committee shall appoint new officers and members-at-large if vacancies occur (Art. 7.7). Empty Judicial Committee seats go to its remaining members (Art. 8.1).

No Judicial Committee election has ever gone to a second ballot, and no at-large election did before 2026, when it took a suspension of the rules. Every earlier shortfall ended another way: a plurality or top-five suspension, a coin toss, or seats left empty. In the 2026 second round, the leader, Steven Nekhaila, had 255 50.00%: exactly half, one ballot short of a majority.

Still Open

Three Questions The Record Doesn’t Settle

Was A Second Ballot Already Required?

Reading 1 or Reading 2 above. The chair took the first. Two motions to suspend the rules failed and an appeal on a third attempt went the chair’s way, but each asked for a two-thirds exception, not whether one was needed; the appeals that would have put that question were ruled dilatory or never put. Moellman himself writes: Clarity codified into the Convention Rules, in favor of either position, would be appreciated.

Rule 8.2 · Art. 16 · RONR 46:32 · Moellman p.5

Are Never-Filled Seats “Vacancies”?

Art. 8.1 lets the remaining members fill vacancies. Robert’s treats an unfinished election as one to finish: the election is taken up automatically at the next regular meeting.

Art. 8.1 · RONR 46:44

Can Two Act Where Five Is A Quorum?

any five members shall constitute a quorum, yet two members made all five appointments. The Bylaws don’t say what happens when fewer than a quorum remain.

Art. 8.1

The Judicial Committee can’t hear a challenge to its own makeup; it isn’t among its seven matters. The forum is the next convention, where an action that conflicts with the bylaws can be raised as a continuing breach, long after the fact RONR 23:6.
