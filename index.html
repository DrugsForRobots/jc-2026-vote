How The Rules Connect

Where The Rules Run Out

The Judicial Committee has no voting procedure of its own. It borrows the at-large one, and that one stops after a single ballot.

Bylaws Art. 8.1

Seven Members, Elected Every Four Years

The Judicial Committee shall be composed of seven Party members elected at each regular non-presidential convention.

Convention Rule 9

Borrow The At-Large Method

Elections run in the same manner as specified for at-large members of the National Committee.

Convention Rule 8.2

One Approval Ballot, A Majority Of Ballots To Win

Each delegate votes for any number of candidates. The top five candidates receiving a majority vote of the ballots cast shall be elected.

The Gap

Rule 8.2 Says Nothing About A Second Ballot

Officers get runoffs written into Rule 8.1c, and presidential nominees into Rule 7.2. At-large and Judicial Committee seats get no such sentence.

Reading 1 · The Chair’s

Rule 8.2 Is Complete. Another Ballot Needs 2/3 To Suspend The Rules.

The minutes record Chair Nekhaila’s ruling on the second-round point of order as NOT WELL TAKEN on the basis that no such requirement exists in the bylaws. On the recording, he says Nope. I'm I'm ruling that we don't. The 2020 Bylaws Committee read it the same way, describing a lack of a procedure to automatically move to additional rounds of voting without a need to suspend the rules.

2026 Minutes p.32 · Convention Recording 4:22 · 2020 Minutes p.174
Reading 2 · Robert’s Fills The Gap

Art. 16 Applies Robert’s, & Robert’s Repeats The Ballot.

Where LP rules are silent, Robert’s governs, and if any office remains unfilled after the first ballot, the balloting is repeated for that office as many times as necessary to obtain a majority vote for a single candidate. An election should be completed at the session at which it is taken up, unless it is impossible or impractical to do so.

Art. 16 · RONR 46:32, 46:44

Precedent: in 2014, the first election under this rule, four LNC at-large candidates won majorities and two tied for the fifth seat at 45.48%. The delegates filled it themselves: Mr. Redpath moved to suspend the requirements of Convention Rule 8.2 and to instead fill the fifth at-large position by coin toss between Doug Craig and Guy McLendon, though they did not receive majority votes; the motion passed on a voice vote. 2014 Minutes p.43

Earlier Conventions

Twenty Years Of Seating Both Races

Rule 9 runs the Judicial Committee election in the same manner as specified for at-large members of the National Committee, so the two races belong side by side. Both elect every seat at once: all five at-large seats at each regular convention, every two years (Art. 7.2–7.3, Art. 10.1), and all seven Judicial Committee seats at each non-presidential one (Art. 8.1). Empty at-large seats go to the LNC: The National Committee shall appoint new officers and members-at-large if vacancies occur (Art. 7.7). Empty Judicial Committee seats go to its remaining members (Art. 8.1).

No Judicial Committee election has ever gone to a second ballot, and no at-large election did before 2026, when it took a suspension of the rules. Every earlier shortfall ended another way: a plurality or top-five suspension, a coin toss, or seats left vacant at adjournment. In the 2026 second round, the leader, Steven Nekhaila, had 255 50.00%: exactly half, one ballot short of a majority.

New Hampshire: Affirmed 7–0

On August 17 the committee upheld the LNC’s disaffiliation of the New Hampshire party: it AFFIRMS the revocation of affiliate party status from the Libertarian Party of New Hampshire by a vote of 7 to 0. New Hampshire and amicus filers argued the appointments by which this Committee filled its vacancies were irregular. The opinion: It was argued by an amicus filer, and by the appellant in their final filings and during oral hearing. Two accepted briefs raised it: Hector Roos, Whether the present Judicial Committee was lawfully constituted, and Nickolas Ciesielski, whether the Committee, as presently composed, was validly constituted at all. Two answered them: Caryn Harlos, Roos argues that the JC is illegitimate and then asks such a purportedly illegitimate JC to rule itself illegitimate, and Jonathan McGee, on the committee’s list of matters it may hear, The Judicial Committee’s own composition is not among them. Moellman’s opinion answered: This Committee cannot rule upon its own legitimacy. The decision as to legitimacy is left to the next convention delegates to determine. Moulton’s concurrence: With no JC, there can be no appeal.

Are Never-Filled Seats “Vacancies”?

Art. 8.1 lets the remaining members fill vacancies. Robert’s treats an unfinished election as one to finish: the election is taken up automatically at the next regular meeting. The next paragraph goes further: If, for any reason, the assembly does not complete an election at the time for which it was scheduled, it should do so as soon as possible and may do so at any time until the expiration of the term the election is to fill. In the meantime, a vacancy in office arises, but Once the election is completed, however, the person elected replaces anyone who filled the vacancy. And: Failure to hold or to complete an election at the scheduled time does not deprive the membership of its right to elect an officer of its choice. The word vacancies was first used from the chair, announcing the results: Those two winners get to fill the rest of the vacancies on the JC. Monday’s resolution used it too, leaving five vacancies, and recited that Art. 8.1 has the remaining members of the Judicial Committee and not the delegates fill any vacant positions on the Judicial Committee.

Art. 8.1 · RONR 46:44 · RONR 46:45 · Convention Recording 3:57 · 2026 Minutes pp.46–47

Can Two Act Where Five Is A Quorum?

any five members shall constitute a quorum, yet two members made all five appointments. The Bylaws don’t say what happens when fewer than a quorum remain.

Moellman answers that Each appointment was agreed upon by both of the members the delegates had elected, via email ballot. and that An electronic mail ballot is not a meeting. In 2016, three elected members, Moulton among them, posted to the LNC business list that we have the authority to fill vacancies, although we are less than the quorum of five specified in the bylaws. and voted unanimously by email ballot to fill the vacancies with Michael Badnarik, John Buttrick, Bill Hall, and Rob Latham. Robert’s Rules, as Moellman quotes them: If, instead, the members present take action informally in the absence of a quorum, they do so at their own risk.

His paper gives no date for the 2026 ballots and doesn’t say who sent them out. O’Brien and Moellman announced the appointments Sunday, May 31.
